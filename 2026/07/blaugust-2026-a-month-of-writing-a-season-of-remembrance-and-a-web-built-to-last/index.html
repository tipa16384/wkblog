Blaugust 2026: a month of writing, a season of remembrance, and a web built to last

July 22, 2026 in Blogging

August’s festival of blogging returns with a different kind of energy this year: determination braided with grief. Multiple organizers and long‑time participants frame Blaugust 2026 as both celebration and tribute after the community’s recent loss of Mark “Belghast” Temple, the event’s founder, with mentors stepping up to carry the torch in his memory Aywren on Aywren's Nook Wilhelm on The Ancient Gaming Noob. That impulse to keep the hearth burning animates personal remembrances too: one writer recalls peers who shaped the hobbyist blogosphere and publicly recommits to posting through August as a tribute to them and to Bel Scarybooster on Scarybooster.

What Blaugust means in 2026

Across this week’s posts, veterans keep returning to two themes: lower the bar to entry, and widen the circle. The event that began as a “31 posts in 31 days” ironman for gamers has mellowed into a broader, inclusive month built around achievable goals and cross‑pollination Wilhelm on The Ancient Gaming Noob. There are still milestone badges—from Newbie through Rainbow Diamond—for those chasing counts, but the heartbeat is community, not a scoreboard Magi on Indiecator. That’s underlined in rallying calls that insist Blaugust isn’t a contest and that the only real way to lose is to sit it out Aywren on Aywren's Nook. The headcount hasn’t hurt that message: participation topped 150 last year, a rejoinder to anyone declaring blogging “dead” Juhis on Hamatti.

How to actually make it through a month of posts

Practical advice is as generous as the invitations. One veteran recommends “winging it” unless you’re chasing the full 31, and suggests keeping a couple of pre‑written safety drafts plus a repeatable feature to lean on when inspiration dips. Theme weeks and fresh prompt lists are already circulating to spark ideas, but matching your usual cadence may be healthier than forcing daily posts Bhagpuss on Inventory Full. Others plan to follow the weekly topic schedule as scaffolding—some still aim for 31, some won’t—and remind newcomers that the point is to keep writing in company, not to obey rules Wilhelm on The Ancient Gaming Noob Juhis on Hamatti. For those who prefer to engage horizontally, the “Community Builder” path returns with challenges like collaborating, guest posting, and highlighting others’ work—an alternative set of achievements designed to make participation a team sport Magi on Indiecator.

The AI line: assist, don’t replace

Organizers are clear that unedited LLM output isn’t welcome, while tools that help you proof, brainstorm, or illustrate remain in bounds Wilhelm on The Ancient Gaming Noob. A complementary, detailed guidance lays out acceptable assistance, disclosure norms, and a simple rule of thumb: AI may assist your creative work, but shouldn’t replace your creative contribution Magi on Indiecator. Lived examples help: one blogger describes using Gemini to clean up Blogger’s flaky HTML and fix a stubborn image‑centering glitch—grunt work offloaded so the human could keep writing—without outsourcing the post itself Bhagpuss on Inventory Full.

Under the hood, the open web is the story

If Blaugust is the surface, RSS and open formats are the current beneath it. One developer argues for “textcasting”—text that moves like podcasts do—via small, interoperable networks built on RSS and friendly to WordPress’ existing hooks, a bootstrap approach meant to resist lock‑in by design Dave Winer on Scripting News. On the consumption side, hard numbers back up the intuition that feeds still matter: over a recent four‑day slice, 88% of traffic to one blog was fetching the RSS feed; most of the remaining web hits were crawlers, with a minority of humans arriving directly or via search. The top readers in that sample were NetNewsWire, FreshRSS, Miniflux, Feedly, and NewsBlur—another reason to format for feed readers first UltrViolet on Endgame Viable. Even ghosts linger in the feed: an exploration of Feedly’s “unreachable” bins turned up long‑vanished sites whose full posts live on in cached RSS, an accidental archive that makes the case for publishing full‑text feeds (and against partials that strand history behind dead links) Bhagpuss on Inventory Full.

Owning your words, climbing at your pace

That archival lesson pairs neatly with an on‑ramp philosophy for the IndieWeb. Rather than a purity test, think of a ladder: start on an easy platform if that gets you writing, then move to your own domain and hand‑rolled HTML, and only later reach for static site generators or servers—progressive disclosure to avoid overwhelming newcomers and to ensure portability when platforms inevitably disappear Brennan on brennan.day. Meanwhile, some veterans are simply sprucing up the homes they have: a Squarespace blog gets a long‑wanted post counter and a more robust, feed‑savvy blogroll—complete with a matrix of latest posts—to be ready for the August rush Roger on Contains Moderate Peril. And for anyone glamorizing the writing life, one candid diary reminds us what Blaugust is actually training: the habit. Daily 750‑word drafts at ungodly hours, modest rituals, a publish‑and‑exhale rhythm—sustainable precisely because it’s ordinary Brennan on brennan.day.

The invitation

If you’ve been waiting for a nudge, this is it. Veterans are already posting welcome mats, info posts, and invites to the community Discord; you can follow weekly themes or ignore them, chase badges or just check in twice. The shared mantra resurfaces across these posts: show up, write something, and let the rest take care of itself Aywren on Aywren's Nook Juhis on Hamatti.
